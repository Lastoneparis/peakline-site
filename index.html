Peakline · ski & snowboard

Start it at the first lift. Don’t touch it again until the bar.

Peakline works out on its own which parts of your day were runs and which were lifts, from GPS and the iPhone’s barometric altimeter. There is nothing to tap at the top of a piste with gloves on.

Days accumulate into a season: totals, personal records, and badges for the things that are actually hard — the biggest single vertical day, the fastest run, the longest streak of days on snow.

Offline first, and that is not a slogan

Coverage in a valley is whatever it is. Peakline records the whole day locally and needs no connection at any point — not to start, not to detect runs, not to save. There is no account and no sign-in.

What it does not do

Read this before your first day

There are no resort piste maps. Licensing them is expensive and Peakline does not pretend to have done it — you get your own track on a standard map, not a named run with a difficulty colour.

There is no social layer: no friends, no feed, no leaderboard against strangers. Records are your own.

Run detection is inference. A long flat traverse, a very short lift or a gondola that climbs slowly can be misread, and altitude from a barometer drifts with the weather. Peakline lets you correct a day afterwards rather than insisting it was right.

Tracking a full day uses GPS continuously, which costs battery. Cold costs more.
